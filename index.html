Room 1 of 6 · At GAL Global Advisory

Secretariat: compliance system

A corporate secretarial firm can't miss a filing deadline or lose a client's file. I designed this system for GAL Global Advisory, the firm I work at, so neither can happen.

🛡️ Four rules it can't break

🗑️Never deletes a fileIt can only add. Every change is a new, dated file.
🧾Every action leaves a recordThe client's folder is a full history, even without the app.
🔒No client data in the codeEvery change is scanned for client names and IDs, and refused if one appears.
🤐No tipping offLetters never reveal screening results or risk ratings.

🧪 How it's tested

  • Deadlines, files, templates and encryption are tested almost line by line, on a separate copy of fictional companies marked TEST.
  • Every upgrade: back up, verify, install, confirm record counts match — or restore and stop.
  • A new version ships only after the full test suite passes three times in a row.

✋ A rule I set

A calculated deadline nobody has checked is a guess. Each rule shows its ACRA source and stays “Not yet” until a person checks it against a real filing.

Northstar: from trading system to ETF tracker

I built a trading system, tested it honestly, and found that simply holding the shares beat it. What survived is a tracker for regular ETF investing.

🗓️ How it went

  1. 🧭
    Aug 2026Northstar — the daily screen

    Fixed rules over large US shares. Every decision checkable.

  2. ⚡

    A same-day strategy passed 27 of 29 tests on unseen data.

  3. 🧪
    Mid-SepRunning on a practice account

    Real orders, pretend money.

  4. ⚠️
    18 SepThe first doubt

    A rebuilt test showed it relied on a number only known after the market closed.

  5. 🔁
    19 SepConfirmed, twice

    Without that number, profit became loss — and real fees were four times what was assumed.

  6. ✅
    21 SepThe tests had the last word

    No strategy reached real money: it all stayed on the practice account.

  7. 📊
    30 SepBecame an ETF tracker

    Seven markets, the US and six in Asia: which index each one follows, and which funds track it.

  8. 🗄️

🎯 The result

Simply holding a market fund beat everything I built.

Five more searches tested over 650 rule sets, fund-switching schemes and a self-learning model. Each was held to the same fairness check against random decisions, and simply holding came out ahead every time.

Room 5 of 6 · Personal project

World news radar

A board that reads news outlets around the world and shows which stories are spreading. Plain code, no AI, $0 to run. Below: twelve of the biggest stories from its first week.

⚙️ How it works

  1. 🕔
    Reads every 5 minutes

    In my app. This page shows a frozen week of its top stories. Headlines and links only, from each outlet's public feed.

  2. 🔗
    Groups them into stories

    Headlines that share the same unusual words become one story.

  3. 📊
    Scores each story

    More outlets, more regions and fresh pick-ups push it up the board.

  4. 🔎
    Matches what people search

    Google Trends from 38 countries shows what's being searched but not yet reported.

🏷️ The badges

🔥 Surging3 or more outlets picked it up in the last 2 hours (in the app only)
📈 Trendingpeople are searching for it on Google
🆕 Newfirst seen in the last hour (in the app only)
🌏 Non-Westernevery outlet carrying it is outside the West
📍 Maybe elsewherea wire dateline suggests it happened in another country

Two more badges, 🔄 Update and ✅ Covered, came from my news channel's files and were retired with it in Oct 2026.

🤝 Rules it keeps
  • A site that says no is respected. Outlets that block automated readers, have no feed or stopped updating are listed in Sources with the reason, never worked around.
  • Stories filed in the right country. Where a wire dateline exists, it moves the story to where it happened.
  • No made-up links. A search term only links to an article when Google gives a real one.
⚠️ Known flaws
  • The country can be wrong. Stories are filed by which countries' outlets carried them; most have no dateline to check.
  • Stories can merge or split. Headlines are grouped by shared words, so two events can become one story, or one event two.
  • One wire story can look like many. Several outlets running the same AP or AFP report each count as an outlet.
  • Headlines only. It never reads the articles, so it can't tell whether a headline is accurate.
  • Gaps in coverage. Some outlets can't be read (see Sources); China, TikTok and Reddit aren't covered.
  • This page is a frozen week. The app itself keeps reading every 5 minutes; this selection doesn't change.

Walk-In Studio

A side project built for fun: a design system and website templates for small shops — bakeries, tailors, nail studios, aircon, restaurants, tuition and accounting. The whole thing is kept here: open the showroom and try every demo.

🧩 What I built

  • A reusable design system and seven trade kits, one layout recipe per trade.
  • A library of demo sites for made-up shops, most in four styles: a bakery menu board, a nail lookbook, an aircon price estimator, a tax-deadline helper.
  • A scroll-driven walk-in hero: scrolling walks you from the street through the shop door.
  • Sixteen Signature demos: the product as the star, or a walk through the shop (drawn, or filmed with stock footage), for every trade.

🧪 A quality harness, not just tests

My checks used to ask “is it broken?” and never “is it good?”. So every page is screenshotted at four sizes and scored 1⁠–⁠5 on nine points by a blind review step that only sees the screenshots, the rubric and reference sites.

📐 TemplatesSix of the nine points must score 4 or more, the other three at least 3.
🏁 Finished sitesA stricter bar: every point must score 4 or more.

✨ Motion as a choice

Standard: pages appear at once. Smooth: eased page changes and gentle reveals. Signature: story-driven motion for one shop. Every template has a Standard / Smooth switch, and the room changes in this walkthrough use the same timing scale.
