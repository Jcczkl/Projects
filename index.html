  • Pass criteria written down and saved before any search ran — never lowered after.

Decide what counts as success after seeing the results, and almost anything looks like a win.

Simple patterns in public price data are the most crowded corner of the market. Firms with more of these found them first:

  • Speed — servers inside the exchange.

A clean negative from a careful process is the process working.

Stories filed in the right countryA US story once showed under Singapore. Where a wire dateline exists, it now moves the story to where it happened.
No made-up linksA search term only links to an article when Google gives a real one.
  • The country can be wrong. Stories are filed by which countries' outlets carried them, not where they happened. Most have no dateline to check: on 6 Oct, only 4 of 294 did.
  • Stories can merge or split. Headlines are grouped by shared words, so two different events can end up as one story, and one event worded differently can show up twice.
  • One wire story can look like many. When several outlets run the same AP or AFP report, each counts as its own outlet, so a story can look bigger than it is.
  • Headlines only. It never reads the articles, so it can't tell whether a headline is accurate.
  • Search matches are word-based. A Google search term is linked to a story by shared words, so a common term can match the wrong story, or none.
  • Gaps in coverage. Some outlets can't be read (see Sources), AP and Reuters only arrive through other outlets, and China, TikTok and Reddit aren't covered.
  • This page is slower than my app. It updates hourly and can run late, some outlets may refuse GitHub's servers, and two badges need my own files, so they only appear in my app.
